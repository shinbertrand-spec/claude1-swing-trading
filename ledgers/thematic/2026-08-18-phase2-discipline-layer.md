### R4 and the §2.4 acceptance test — the design was redesigned, and here is why

**A bare capex-vs-revenue ratio would NOT have flagged the calibration event.** Neither the 2026-08-03 Texas pause nor the 2026-08-11 EIA cut moves capex guidance or disclosed AI revenue in-quarter — the ratio sits still while physical demand is revised underneath it. ## 3. Where the schema fought me (report item 3)

1. **"One variable" vs the acceptance test.** The handoff asks for ONE portfolio-level variable, but the event it must catch never touches capex or revenue prints. The resolution — one composite status fed by three channels — is honest, but it means "one variable" is really "one flag." Stated rather than papered over.
2. **Channel B was already firing at arm time.** The monetisation de-rate is the audit's own evidence, so arming a threshold on it from today's marks required a choice: fire retroactively (noise) or re-baseline (loses the signal that motivated the monitor). I re-baselined and recorded the status as ELEVATED with the history in the file. A monitor armed *after* its leading indicator moved cannot claim the early warning; it can only catch the *next* leg.
3. **`edge_claim_if_covered` wants to be filled with a story.** Writing FIX's honest answer ("none documented") took active resistance — every draft wanted to argue duration-judgment as an edge. The field works precisely because a blank blocks candidacy and a story has to be *claimed* explicitly, where the quarterly re-verification can attack it.
4. **The cooldown between R1 and R2.** E1 (an entry trigger) and the invalidation triggers share cadence dates but have opposite failure modes: checking E1 off-cadence on a drawdown is *fine* (entering cold is the point), while checking sells off-cadence is *prohibited*. The template now carries that asymmetry — entry checks are "quarterly + event-driven on drawdowns," sell evaluation is scheduled-only.
